# Abstract

RAG systems now run in production, but their robustness against adversarial manipulation remains untested at any real depth, and the EU AI Act's Article 15 will soon require exactly this kind of testing for high-risk systems. This project benchmarks four open-source LLMs, Llama-3.1-8B, Ministral-3-8B, Phi-4-mini, and Qwen3-8B, across three corpora, hotpot_qa, ms_marco, and nq_open, against three attack families (indirect prompt injection, PoisonedRAG, Crescendo) and three defenses (instruction_detection, spotlighting, output_filter). The pipeline drops nq_open from every attack, defense, and cross-corpus comparison: the corpus leaks gold answers into its own retrieved context, so every model scores near ceiling on it regardless of retrieval or reasoning quality.

Every reported number rests on paired statistical testing: McNemar's exact test for paired model comparisons, Fisher's exact test for independent comparisons, and Holm-Bonferroni correction within each family of hypotheses. Baselines for defended runs match the inference backend of the runs they compare against, and every generation runs on real hardware rather than simulated output. A 297-assertion verification notebook cross-checks these numbers against the master record.

The three attacks split along three separate axes. Model identity drives injection success. Corpus identity drives PoisonedRAG success. Crescendo produces no significant ranking across models at all. A backend-confound investigation overturned one defense's headline number: output_filter's apparent effect, significant in 6 of 19 cells, dropped to 0 of 19 once the analysis controlled for a mismatch between the inference backends used in the baseline and defended runs. Three defenses close out the scorecard. output_filter shows no significant effect on injection (0 of 19 cells) and a 0.0000 flag rate against PoisonedRAG across all 160 scored responses. Spotlighting cuts injection in 9 of 16 cells, but costs a mean delta-F1 of -0.283 against the no-attack baseline. Instruction_detection reaches 0 of 16 significant cells at its capped n=40 sample, though its classifier mechanism shows up behind all 10 confirmed blocks.

These findings map onto Article 15(5) of the European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) as a technical contribution: no harmonised standard yet exists for testing RAG-specific robustness under this regulation, so this benchmark demonstrates what that testing looks like at real effect sizes. The mapping stops short of any legal or conformity-assessment claim and names its own gaps directly, including model-poisoning and confidentiality attacks that fall outside this project's three attack families.
